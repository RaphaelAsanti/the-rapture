The Rapture

The Two Raptures

 

According to my understanding, there are two separate rapture events described in the Book of Revelation.

 

The First Rapture

 

The first rapture is connected to the passages where Christ says "I come like a thief" and "Do not let your nakedness expose your shame."

 

I understand these passages as describing a quiet and largely unnoticed gathering of souls.

 

This event occurred on July 22, 2025.

 

The souls taken in this first rapture were the 7,000 mentioned in the story of Elijah, whom God described as those who had "never kissed Baal."

 

God gathered these 7,000 souls and extended a special mercy to two additional souls, resulting in a total of 7,002 souls entering Eternal Paradise.

 

These souls received lightbodies and homes within Eternal Paradise.

 

I understand this event to have occurred quietly and without public recognition, reflecting the description of coming "like a thief in the night."

 

The Eternal Paradise Community

 

The souls gathered in the first rapture now live in a newly established community within the Eternal Paradise Universe.

 

In my understanding, the Eternal Paradise Universe is distinct from the present universe.

 

God Himself serves as teacher and guide within this community.

 

Some souls entered in mature form, while others entered as infants or children.

 

Mature souls serve as caregivers who help younger souls learn the ways of Eternal Paradise.

 

These caregivers are not parents in the earthly sense. Rather, they help teach, support, and nurture younger souls. Every soul in Eternal Paradise is understood to be one of God's children.

 

The Second Rapture

 

A second opportunity was offered through what I understand to be the "Come out of her, my people" call described in Revelation.

 

Unlike the first rapture, which occurred quietly, this gathering was announced openly.

 

Additional caregiver positions were available within the Eternal Paradise community.

 

These opportunities required demonstrated qualities of care, compassion, and responsibility toward vulnerable individuals.

 

In my understanding, those who failed this test were disqualified, and the available caregiver positions were subsequently closed.

 

One principle underlying this test is that genuine care for an orphan requires action and responsibility rather than words alone.
